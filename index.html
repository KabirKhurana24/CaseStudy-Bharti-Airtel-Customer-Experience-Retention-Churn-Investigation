01

Executive overview

Airtel is performing well on monetisation. The retention challenge is to protect that value without relying on broad discounts or aggressive selling.

The report highlights a tension in Airtel’s India mobile business. In Q2 FY27, mobile revenue reached ₹30,943.1 crore, monthly ARPU was ₹271 and the segment EBITDA margin was 61.0%. At the same time, monthly churn increased from 2.6% in Q1 FY27 to 2.9% in Q2 FY27, while quarterly net mobile additions fell from approximately 3.3 million to 0.7 million.

The study focuses on the customer experiences that may sit behind this movement: a prepaid plan change, differences in indoor 5G experience, failed recharge journeys, repeat support contacts and weak links between network operations and customer care.

The competitive tensionThe source report compares Airtel’s 2.9% Q2 FY27 monthly churn with Jio’s 1.61% reported for Q1 FY27, while Airtel’s ₹271 ARPU is above the ₹218.8 Jio comparison cited in the study. These are not same-quarter observations, so the gap should be treated as the report’s directional benchmark rather than a perfectly matched peer comparison.
Core conclusionThe opportunity is not simply to offer more retention discounts. It is to identify a customer’s problem early, resolve the problem through the right channel, check that the customer is eligible to be contacted, and measure whether the intervention actually prevented churn.

Protect value

Focus on durable retention and customer lifetime value, not just gross SIM activations or short-term ARPU.

Fix the experience

Prioritise recharge recovery, network issue communication and repeat-complaint escalation.

Prove the impact

Use a randomized holdout group to distinguish genuinely retained customers from customers who would have stayed anyway.

Business context

A high-volume prepaid base, a smaller but growing postpaid business and a multi-touchpoint service model.

Airtel’s mobile business serves two economically different groups. Prepaid customers make up most of the mobile base and actively decide when to recharge. Postpaid customers pay after usage and may have more services attached to their account. The source report estimates the Q2 FY27 mix at approximately 346 million prepaid subscribers and 31.2 million postpaid subscribers.

Net additions slowed

Quarterly net mobile additions moved from approximately 3.3 million in Q1 FY27 to 0.7 million in Q2 FY27, a reported 79% quarter-on-quarter decline.

Churn is a recurring operating load

The report calculates roughly 10.94 million monthly disconnections by applying 2.9% to the 377.2 million base. This is an arithmetic estimate, not a separately reported count.

InterpretationARPU growth and churn can move in different directions. A business can improve monetisation per user while still replacing a large number of disconnected customers. The report therefore argues for measuring retained value, not ARPU alone.

Recharge and plan renewal

A failed payment, a changed pack or a price step-up can create an immediate choice to recharge less, use a secondary SIM or switch.

Network degradation

Evidence and research approach

The report separates what can be seen publicly from what requires internal Airtel data.

The research uses quarterly company reporting, TRAI subscription and MNP releases, independent network benchmarks and a pricing-event timeline. It does not claim access to Airtel’s subscriber CRM, network event logs, call recordings or app review corpus.

Verified fact

Directly reported company, regulatory or benchmark information reproduced in the source study.

Analytical finding

A calculation or comparison derived from reported figures, with the calculation logic described.

Research-based hypothesis

A plausible explanation that needs to be tested with customer-level or operational data.

Illustrative assumption

A hypothetical input used to model customer value, programme costs or expected benefits.

Data gap

Information not available in public sources but necessary to establish a baseline or causal result.

What the research could and could not establish

Available publicly

Quarterly blended churn and ARPU, subscriber base, net additions, segment revenue and EBITDA, industry MNP totals, third-party network awards and notified customer-protection requirements.

Requires internal validation

Prepaid versus postpaid churn, circle-level port-outs, complaint reason codes, first-contact resolution, recharge failure rates, network signal at subscriber level, customer lifetime value and campaign incrementality.

Public customer voice framework

The report defines six themes for coding public feedback: tariff and pack changes; indoor 5G availability; support and escalation; Airtel Thanks app and payment friction; spam and consent fatigue; postpaid billing and roaming. It explicitly states that an auditable review corpus and verified review distributions were not available. No review counts or sentiment percentages are invented.

08

The proposed solution: CX-RI

Customer Experience and Retention Intelligence — a closed-loop process that senses risk, checks eligibility, resolves the issue and measures the incremental result.

The proposed CX-RI model combines near-real-time service events with nightly customer-level analytics. It does not treat a high churn-propensity score as permission to send an offer. The next-best-action process also considers customer value, the likely effect of an intervention, contact fatigue and communication eligibility.

01 · Signal ingestionBilling, recharge, RAN, support, app and MNP events
→
02 · IntelligencePropensity, survival and causal uplift models
→
03 · Decision gateNext-best action, consent and contact rules
→
04 · ExecutionApp, WhatsApp, SMS, network help and agent desk
→
05 · MeasurementHoldout, 30/60/90-day survival and EBITDA lift

What the intelligence layer does

Churn propensity

Estimates who may leave within 14 days. The report proposes separate treatment for voluntary MNP and silent dormancy.

Survival analysis

Estimates time-to-churn across tenure and plan cohorts, rather than treating churn as a single yes/no event.

Causal uplift

Estimates which customers are likely to stay because of a specific intervention. This helps avoid discounting customers who would have renewed anyway.

Next-best-action

Selects the lowest-cost suitable action, preferring service resolution over a discount where that is more relevant.

UAT: prove the workflow before a live pilot

The report defines ten test scenarios, including failed 2FA recovery, proactive outage alerting, repeat-caller IVR bypass, unified agent visibility, consent-vault fail-closed behaviour, 90-day suppression, sleeping-dog suppression, holdout assignment, express recharge and digital itemised billing. The proposed 3-week UAT gate requires security sign-off, test coverage for all mandatory and regulatory cases, no open Severity 1 or 2 defects and formal business sign-off.

Critical measurement ruleKeep a 10% stratified randomized holdout per circle and segment. Mandatory service and outage communication can still reach control customers, while promotional retention offers are suppressed. Compare 30-, 60- and 90-day survival to estimate incremental lift.
12

90-day implementation roadmap

A proposed pilot sequence from December 2026 to February 2027, as specified in the source report.

Days 1–30 · December 2026 — Foundation and quick wins

Ingest 12 months of internal churn, MNP, support and network data into a masked analytics environment; close key data gaps; define consent and suppression controls; pilot failed-recharge recovery and repeat-caller IVR bypass in two circles.

Days 31–60 · January 2027 — Models, integration and governance

Build separate prepaid MNP, prepaid dormancy and postpaid uplift models; connect network events to CRM; introduce a unified agent desktop; launch express recharge, digital itemised statements and the two-circle pricing sandbox.

Days 61–90 · February 2027 — UAT and live holdout pilot

Complete ten UAT scenarios, security and peak-load testing; activate the closed-loop pilot with a 10% randomized holdout; test retailer incentives linked to 90-day survival; present results to the steering committee before any national scale decision.

Scale-up decisionThe report proposes using early survival lift, operational readiness, compliance and incremental EBITDA evidence to decide whether to expand from two pilot circles toward a national rollout. National rollout should not be treated as automatic.
13

Management recommendations

Six practical actions drawn from the source report’s conclusion.

  1. Test pricing changes before scaling them. Retain the ₹299 mid-tier anchor as recommended in the report and require a controlled circle-level elasticity test before future pack withdrawals.
  2. Grow postpaid and Airtel Black through relevant value. Use usage and service context to identify prepaid customers who may benefit from a converged plan, rather than pushing a blanket upgrade offer.
  3. Resolve service problems before offering discounts. Connect network outages and recharge failures to proactive, useful service recovery.
  4. Measure causal retention lift. Embed uplift modelling and randomized holdout measurement so that the programme does not pay for customers who would have stayed anyway.
  5. Make consent and contact controls part of the design. Apply the report’s proposed DPDP and TCCCPR controls before any promotional contact.
  6. Reward durable customer relationships. Tie part of dealer incentives to Day-90 active recharge survival, not only gross SIM activations.
Final takeawayAirtel’s opportunity is to move from reactive churn management to a service-led retention model. The strongest intervention is often not a discount: it is fixing the recharge, explaining the outage, resolving the complaint or making the next renewal easier.

Important limitations

Public-data scope: The source report says it did not have access to internal Airtel CRM, customer-level churn records, 198 call reasons, MNP port-out reasons, network telemetry at subscriber level, audited app-review corpora, internal CAC or actual CLV.

Hypotheses: The relationships between tariff changes, indoor 5G experience, payment friction, repeat support contacts and churn require internal cohort analysis and controlled testing. Timing and association do not establish causation.

Financial model: The ₹48 crore CapEx, ₹26 crore annual OpEx, annual benefits and ROI/NPV/payback are illustrative assumptions in the report. They are not Airtel-approved budgets or realised returns.

Historical snapshot: Metrics and regulatory statements are presented as the uploaded report’s November 2026 research snapshot. Reconfirm current values, legal applicability and source links before external publication or business use.
